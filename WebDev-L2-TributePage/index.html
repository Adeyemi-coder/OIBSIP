01 Biography

A life shaped by questioning

Wole Soyinka was born on 13 July 1934 in Abeokuta, in western Nigeria. After studying at the University of Leeds he spent six years in England, working as a dramaturg at London’s Royal Court Theatre, where he wrote his first plays. In 1960 a Rockefeller bursary brought him home to study African drama, just as Nigeria was becoming independent.

He came back with a clear idea of what theatre could do. That year he founded The 1960 Masks, and in 1964 the Orisun Theatre Company. For the independence celebrations he wrote A Dance of the Forests, a play that treated the moment as a time for scrutiny as much as celebration. His work draws on Yoruba mythology, with Ogun, the god of iron and war, at its centre, and joins it to dance, music and the traditions of European drama.

Held for twenty-two months, he turned detention into literature.

His convictions cost him his freedom. In 1967 he published an appeal for a cease-fire in the Nigerian Civil War and was arrested, accused of conspiring with the Biafran side. He was held as a political prisoner for 22 months, until 1969. The Man Died, the poems of A Shuttle in the Crypt and the novel Season of Anomy all grew out of that experience.

Public life has followed him ever since. In 1986 he became the first African to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. In 1994 he left Nigeria under General Sani Abacha’s military rule, and he returned in 1998, after Abacha’s death. Now in his nineties he remains a vocal presence in Nigerian public life, and his writing keeps returning to the same questions: who holds power, and what do they owe the people they rule?

03 Timeline

Selected milestones

A selective chronology, not a complete life record.

  1. 1934

    Born in Abeokuta

    Akinwande Oluwole Babatunde Soyinka is born on 13 July in western Nigeria.

  2. 1958

    The Royal Court Theatre

    After Leeds, he works as a dramaturg at London’s Royal Court Theatre. His first plays are staged in Ibadan.

  3. 1960

    Home for independence

    He returns on a Rockefeller bursary, founds The 1960 Masks and writes A Dance of the Forests for the independence celebrations.

  4. 1967

    Arrested during the civil war

    After appealing for a cease-fire, he is accused of conspiring with the Biafran side and detained as a political prisoner.

  5. 1969

    Released after 22 months

    The experience becomes The Man Died and A Shuttle in the Crypt.

  6. 1975

    Back at Ife

    He returns to Nigeria and becomes professor of comparative literature at the University of Ife. Death and the King’s Horseman is published.

  7. 1986

    Nobel Prize in Literature

    He becomes the first African to win the prize. His lecture, This Past Must Address Its Present, is dedicated to Nelson Mandela.

  8. 1994

    Exile under Abacha

    He leaves Nigeria under General Sani Abacha’s military rule. In 1997 the regime charges him with treason in absentia.

  9. 1998

    Return to Nigeria

    He comes home after Abacha’s death, ending four years in exile.

  10. 2004

    The Reith Lectures

    He delivers the BBC Reith Lectures, later published as Climate of Fear.

  11. 2024

    Honoured at ninety

    On his 90th birthday the federal government announces that Lagos’s National Theatre will be renamed the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts.

Timeline spacing is not to scale.

05 Continuing work

A body of work, still being read

Soyinka’s writing crosses genres and traditions. Reading his plays, poetry, fiction and essays together offers a fuller view of an artist whose work repeatedly examines how people live with power, memory, culture and responsibility.
